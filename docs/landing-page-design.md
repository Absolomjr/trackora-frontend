# Trackora Landing Page — Design & Copy Specification

Public marketing page, served before login. Audience: hardware store owners and
managers in Uganda and similar markets, moving from paper and Excel to digital.

**Tone:** clear, practical, energetic. Concrete outcomes, not buzzwords. No
"innovative solution", no "next-gen platform", no slang.

---

- **Fewer stockouts.** Get a low-stock alert before a customer asks for something you don't have.

**Pain bullets (6):**

### Section 5 — Feature clusters

**Section label:** `What's inside`

**Headline:**
> Everything your shop runs on, in one place.

**Subheadline:**
> Four areas. No add-ons, no modules to buy separately.

---

**Cluster 1 — Inventory & stock control**
*Icon: box / warehouse. Accent: electric blue.*

> Keep an accurate count of every item you sell, and know the moment something
> is about to run out.

- Add products with a photo, SKU, category, unit (piece, bag, kg, metre, roll) and price.
- Set a reorder level per product and get flagged automatically when stock drops below it.
- Record stock coming in from suppliers and stock going out for damage, returns or corrections — every entry permanent and traceable.

---

**Cluster 2 — Sales & orders**
*Icon: receipt / cart. Accent: vivid teal.*

> Serve customers faster at the counter, and keep a clean record of every sale
> without extra paperwork.

- Ring up a sale by picking products and quantities — the total calculates itself.
- Record the payment method (cash, mobile money, card, bank or credit) and attach the sale to a customer, or leave it as walk-in.
- Cancel a wrong order and Trackora returns the stock to the shelf automatically.

---

**Cluster 3 — Reports & insights**
*Icon: chart. Accent: lime.*

> Understand your shop in numbers, so you order and price with confidence
> instead of instinct.

- See daily and monthly sales trends over the last 7, 30 or 90 days.
- Know your revenue, cost of goods, profit and margin — not just what came in.
- See your best-selling products and your low-stock list side by side, so you reorder what actually sells.

---

**Cluster 4 — Roles & staff control**
*Icon: shield / users. Accent: neon coral.*

> Give your team exactly the access they need to do their job, and nothing more.

- Three roles out of the box: admin, manager and staff.
- Attendants can sell and record stock; only managers and admins can change prices, edit products or cancel orders.
- Every stock movement and every order records who made it — so questions have answers.

## 3. Design guidance

### 3.1 Colour palette

Dark-first. The hero, final CTA and roughly half the section bands sit on dark
surfaces; the problem, features and FAQ sections sit on the off-white base for
rhythm.

**Brand accents**
| Token | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `--brand-blue` | `#2563EB` | Primary buttons, links, primary accent |
| `--brand-blue-bright` | `#3B82F6` | Hover state, gradient start |
| `--brand-teal` | `#14B8A6` | Secondary accent, gradient end, chart series 2 |
| `--brand-lime` | `#A3E635` | Money/profit highlights, success, chart series 3 |
| `--brand-coral` | `#FF6B5A` | Alerts, roles cluster, sparing emphasis only |

**Dark surfaces**
| Token | Hex |
|---|---|
| `--dark-900` | `#0A0F1E` (page background) |
| `--dark-800` | `#111827` (section band) |
| `--dark-700` | `#1B2437` (card) |
| `--dark-border` | `#2A3550` |

**Light surfaces**
| Token | Hex |
|---|---|
| `--light-050` | `#F8FAFC` (page background) |
| `--light-000` | `#FFFFFF` (card) |
| `--light-border` | `#E2E8F0` |

**Text**
| Token | Hex |
|---|---|
| `--text-on-dark` | `#F1F5F9` |
| `--text-on-dark-muted` | `#94A3B8` |
| `--text-on-light` | `#0F172A` |
| `--text-on-light-muted` | `#64748B` |

**Semantic (match the app's existing badges — do not invent new ones)**
| Token | Hex | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `--success` | `#16A34A` | In stock, completed |
| `--warning` | `#F59E0B` | Low stock, pending |
| `--danger` | `#DC2626` | Out of stock, cancelled |

**Signature gradient:** `linear-gradient(135deg, #2563EB 0%, #14B8A6 100%)`.
Use it on the hero glow, the final CTA band, and the active step in the stepper.
Nowhere else — a gradient stops being a signature when it's everywhere.

**Contrast:** verify every text/background pair reaches WCAG AA (4.5:1 for body,
3:1 for large text). `--brand-lime` on white fails badly — only use lime on dark
surfaces, or as a fill behind dark text.

### 3.2 Typography

- **Display / headings:** `Space Grotesk` (Google Fonts) — geometric, technical,
  slightly unusual. Weights 500 and 700. Fallback: `"Space Grotesk", "Inter", system-ui, sans-serif`.
- **Body / UI:** `Inter` (Google Fonts). Weights 400, 500, 600. Matches the app.
- **Numerals in stat cards and charts:** Inter with `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`
  so figures don't shift width as they animate.

*Cheaper alternative if you want one family:* Inter throughout, headings at
weight 800 with `letter-spacing: -0.03em`.

**Scale (desktop → mobile):**
| Role | Desktop | Mobile | Weight | Tracking |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hero headline | 64px | 36px | 700 | -0.03em |
| Section headline | 44px | 30px | 700 | -0.02em |
| Cluster headline | 26px | 22px | 600 | -0.01em |
| Subheadline | 20px | 17px | 400 | normal |
| Body | 17px | 16px | 400 | normal |
| Bullet / card body | 16px | 15px | 400 | normal |
| Eyebrow / label | 13px | 12px | 600 | 0.12em, uppercase |
| Button | 16px | 16px | 600 | normal |

Line height: 1.1 for hero, 1.2 for section headings, 1.6 for body. Cap paragraph
width at ~65 characters.

Load only the weights listed, `display=swap`, and self-host or preconnect to
`fonts.gstatic.com`.

### 3.3 Buttons

**Primary** — solid `--brand-blue`, white text, 12px radius, 14px/28px padding,
weight 600. Shadow `0 4px 14px rgba(37,99,235,0.35)`. Hover: `--brand-blue-bright`,
lift 2px, shadow grows. Active: return to 0px lift. Focus: 3px outline in
`--brand-teal` at 2px offset.

**Secondary** — transparent fill, 1.5px border. On dark: border and text in
`--text-on-dark`, hover fills `rgba(255,255,255,0.08)`. On light: border
`--light-border`, text `--text-on-light`, hover fills `--light-050`. Same radius
and padding as primary so pairs align.

**Tertiary / text link** — `--brand-blue`, weight 600, arrow glyph that slides
4px right on hover.

Both hero CTAs sit side by side on desktop with 16px gap; full-width and stacked
on mobile, primary first.

### 3.4 Surfaces

- **Feature card:** 16px radius, 28px padding, 1px border. On dark:
  `--dark-700` fill, `--dark-border` border. On light: white fill,
  `--light-border` border, shadow `0 1px 3px rgba(15,23,42,0.06)`.
- **Glass card (hero overlays only):** `rgba(27,36,55,0.72)` with
  `backdrop-filter: blur(16px)` and a 1px `rgba(255,255,255,0.12)` border.
  Always supply a solid fallback for browsers without `backdrop-filter`.
- **Badges:** reuse the app's `Badge` styling exactly — pill radius, 12px text,
  weight 600, tinted background at ~12% opacity with the solid semantic colour
  as text. Do not restyle them for marketing.
- **Section bands:** alternate dark and light. Give each band 96px vertical
  padding on desktop, 64px on mobile.

### 3.5 Icons

Use `react-icons` — already a dependency. Prefer the **Lucide** set (`react-icons/lu`)
for a consistent 2px-stroke outline look; fall back to Feather (`react-icons/fi`).
Never mix filled and outline sets in the same row. Feature cluster icons sit in a
44px rounded-square tile filled with the cluster accent at 12% opacity, icon
itself at full accent colour — this is the same construction as the app's
`StatCard` icon tile, so reuse that component's styling.

### 3.6 Layout

**Desktop**
- Max content width **1200px**, centred, 24px side gutters. Hero text column
  caps at 640px so the headline breaks in a controlled place.
- 12-column grid, 24px gutters.
- Above the fold at 1440×900: eyebrow, headline, subheadline, benefit bullets,
  both CTAs, and the top ~60% of the hero visual. The trust bar should peek at
  the bottom edge to signal scrollability.
- Hero: two columns, 55% text / 45% visual.
- Feature clusters: 2×2 grid.
- How it works: 4 columns.
- FAQ: single column capped at 760px, centred.
- Footer: 4 columns.

**Mobile (make fully responsive — stack on mobile)**
- Single column throughout, 20px side gutters.
- Hero: text first, visual below, cropped to the dashboard card only — do not
  shrink the full three-card composite into an unreadable thumbnail.
- Benefit bullets become a vertical list with icons at 20px.
- Feature clusters stack 1-up; consider collapsing bullets behind the cluster
  headline as an accordion if the section gets too long.
- How it works becomes a vertical timeline.
- Product preview becomes a horizontal snap-scroll carousel with dot indicators.
- FAQ items are collapsed by default; first item open.
- Both CTAs full-width, stacked, primary on top.
- Tap targets minimum 44×44px.

**Breakpoints:** 640px (sm), 768px (md), 1024px (lg), 1280px (xl).

**Vertical rhythm:** 8px base unit. Section padding 96/64. Headline→subheadline
16px, subheadline→body 24px, body→CTA 32px.

### 3.7 Micro-interactions

Keep all of these cheap — CSS transforms and opacity only, no layout-triggering
properties, no animation library.

- **Card hover:** lift 4px, border brightens to the cluster accent, shadow
  deepens. 180ms `ease-out`.
- **Section reveal:** fade in + 24px rise as each section enters the viewport.
  Use one `IntersectionObserver` for the whole page, trigger once, then
  unobserve. Never animate on scroll position continuously.
- **Stat counters:** count from 0 to the target over ~1.2s with an ease-out
  curve, triggered once when the stat row first becomes visible. Use
  `requestAnimationFrame`, not `setInterval`. Render the final value in the DOM
  first so it's correct without JS.
- **Hero visual:** the three cards fade and rise in with a 120ms stagger on
  mount. Optionally, one very slow (8s) float on the floating alert card.
  Nothing else moves.
- **Chart bars in the preview:** grow from zero height, 60ms stagger, once.
- **FAQ accordion:** height and opacity transition, 200ms, with a chevron that
  rotates 180°.
- **Buttons:** 2px lift and shadow growth, 150ms.
- **Logo strip:** greyscale → colour on hover, 200ms.

**Respect `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`** — disable reveals, counters,
staggers and the float; render everything in its final state immediately. This
is a hard requirement, not a nice-to-have.

**Performance budget:** hero image under 200KB, served as WebP with a low-quality
placeholder. Lazy-load every image below the fold. No third-party scripts on this
page. Target LCP under 2.5s on a 3G connection — a meaningful share of this
audience is on mobile data.

### 3.8 Consistency with the internal app

The landing page should feel like the front door of the app, not a different
product. Reuse rather than reinvent:

- **Reuse directly from `src/components/`:** `Button` (already has variants,
  sizes and a loading state), `Badge` / `StockBadge` (semantic colours must match
  the app exactly), `Input` (for the lead forms — it already handles labels,
  required markers, errors and hints), `Card`, `Spinner`.
- **Mirror, don't import:** `StatCard`'s icon-tile construction for feature
  cluster icons; `DataTable`'s row height, border and header treatment for any
  table shown in a screenshot mockup.
- **Share the design tokens.** Add the palette above to `src/styles/index.css`
  as CSS custom properties and consume them from both the app and the landing
  page. The semantic colours in particular must be defined once — a `Low stock`
  amber that differs between the marketing page and the product is a visible
  crack.
- **Fonts must match.** If the app is on Inter, the landing page body is Inter.
  Only the display font is new.

## 4. Backend wiring (implemented)

Both CTAs are backed by a new `apps.leads` Django app. Registration stays
admin-only — the landing page captures a *request*, an admin reviews it and
provisions the real user through the existing `/api/auth/users/` endpoint.

### Endpoints

| Method | Path | Auth | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| POST | `/api/leads/` | **Public**, throttled | Capture a signup or demo request |
| GET | `/api/leads/all/` | Admin | List leads (filter by `kind`, `status`; search; order) |
| GET/PATCH/DELETE | `/api/leads/all/<id>/` | Admin | Review one lead |

### Request body for `POST /api/leads/`

```json
{
  "kind": "signup",
  "full_name": "Nakabugo Sarah",
  "email": "sarah@example.com",
  "phone": "+256700000000",
  "business_name": "Bugolobi Hardware",
  "location": "Kampala",
  "message": "We have about 1,200 products.",
  "source": "hero"
}
```

- `kind` is `signup` or `demo` (default `signup`).
- `full_name` and `email` are required. A `demo` lead additionally requires
  `phone` or `business_name`.
- `source` should name the landing-page section, so you can see which section
  converts. Pass it through from `CTAButtons`.
- Success returns **201** with `{"detail": "..."}` only — the stored record is
  never echoed back to an anonymous caller.
- Validation failures return **400** with per-field errors, which map directly
  onto the app's existing `Input` error prop.
- The endpoint is throttled to **10 requests/hour per IP**, configurable via the
  `LEAD_THROTTLE_RATE` env var.

### Frontend client

`src/api/leadsApi.js` — `leadsApi.requestAccount(payload)` and
`leadsApi.requestDemo(payload)`. It uses a bare axios instance rather than the
shared `api` client, so a stale token in `localStorage` can't trigger the
401-refresh interceptor on a public page.

### Reviewing leads

Django admin at `/admin/leads/lead/` — filterable by kind and status, with
`status` editable inline from the list view. Contact details are read-only;
only `status` and `note` can be changed. No email notification is configured
(the backend has no SMTP setup at all), so leads must be checked in the admin.
